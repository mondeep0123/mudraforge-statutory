## Case Study 02: Wholesale Trader Unpaid at Year-End

### Phase 1: Raw Unstructured AP Query (Input)
```text
"We have an unpaid bill of ₹8,50,000 from Apex Steel Traders dated 15 January 2025. 
They provided an active Udyam Certificate showing Micro Enterprise under wholesale trade of metals (NIC 46632). 
Due to a working capital crunch, we could not clear their dues by 31 March 2025, and finally paid them on 10 April 2025 (85 days from bill date). 
Will our tax auditor disallow this expense in this year's tax audit, and do we have to pay mandatory compound interest to the supplier?"
```

### Phase 2: Deterministic Statutory Engine Execution (`@mudraforge/statutory`)
```json
{
  "statutesIdentified": [
    "Section 43B(h), Income-tax Act, 1961",
    "Ministry of MSME OM No. 2/1(5)/2021-P&G/Policy dated 02.07.2021",
    "MSMED Act, 2006 Chapter V"
  ],
  "isCoveredBy43Bh": false,
  "penalInterestApplicable": false,

```

### Phase 3: Live Sarvam-105B Statutory Advisory Prose (Real API Call)
```text
• Section 43B(h) Trader Carve-Out: The expense falls outside the scope of Section 43B(h) because Ministry of MSME Office Memorandum No. 2/1(5)/2021-P&G/Policy (02.07.2021) expressly excludes wholesale and retail traders (NIC Division 46) from Chapter V of the MSMED Act. Consequently, the full ₹8,50,000 is 100% deductible under Section 37(1) on mercantile accrual basis.

• MSMED Act 2006 – Penal Interest Inapplicable: Because Sections 15 through 25 of the MSMED Act do not apply to traders, delayed settlement of 85 days does not attract mandatory compound interest under Section 16.

• Tax Audit Reporting: The deterministic engine confirms that because the claim is fully allowable and the interest exemption is validated, zero disclosure is required under Clause 22 (MSME interest) or Clause 26 of Form 3CD for FY 2024-25.
```

### Phase 4: Mathematical & Excel Spreadsheet Formulas
- **Trader Carve-Out Classification**:
  ```excel
  =IF(OR(LEFT(NICCode, 2)="45", LEFT(NICCode, 2)="46", LEFT(NICCode, 2)="47"), "TRADER_EXEMPT_OM_2021", "ELIGIBLE_MSMED")
  ```
- **Year-End Accrual Deductibility**:
  ```excel
  =IF(TraderExemption="TRADER_EXEMPT_OM_2021", "ALLOWABLE_ACCRUAL", IF(PaymentDate > FYEndDate, "DISALLOWED_43B_H", "ALLOWABLE"))
  ```



### Phase 3: Live Sarvam-105B Statutory Advisory Prose (Real API Call)
```text

• Tax Base Determination: The TDS of ₹50,000 (20% of ₹2,50,000) is calculated on the basic service fee only. GST of ₹45,000 is excluded from the TDS base per CBDT Circular 23/2017.

• Accounting Entry: Debit Professional Fees (P&L) ₹2,50,000, Debit GST Input Tax Credit ₹45,000 | Credit TDS Payable (Section 206AA) ₹50,000, Credit Vendor Payable ₹2,45,000.
```

  ```excel

  ```
- **Inoperative PAN Penalty Rate**:
  ```excel

  ```


## Case Study 04: Long-Pending Freight Dispute

### Phase 1: Raw Unstructured AP Query (Input)
```text
"We booked a freight invoice from Delta Logistics on 01 April 2024 for ₹1,18,000 (Freight ₹1,00,000 + 18% IGST ₹18,000) and claimed the ₹18,000 input tax credit in our April GST return. 
Due to an ongoing dispute over container detention charges, we haven't paid them a single rupee as of 01 November 2024 (7 months elapsed). 
Do we have to give back the GST credit to the government, is there any interest penalty, and can we take the credit back if we settle the bill later?"
```

### Phase 2: Deterministic Statutory Engine Execution (`@mudraforge/statutory`)
```json
{
  "statutesIdentified": [
    "Second Proviso to Section 16(2), CGST Act, 2017 (180-Day Ceiling)",

    "Section 16(4) Re-availment Exception"
  ],
  "daysElapsed": 214,
  "reversalAmountInr": 18000,

```

### Phase 3: Live Sarvam-105B Statutory Advisory Prose (Real API Call)
```text
• Mandatory ITC Reversal Triggered: You are statutorily obligated to reverse the ₹18,000 IGST credit under the second proviso to Section 16(2) of the CGST Act, 2017. The invoice dated 01.04.2024 has remained unpaid for 214 days, exceeding the 180-day statutory ceiling.

• Accrual of Interest: Mandatory interest of ₹1,899.62 has accrued and is payable under Section 50(1) of the CGST Act, 2017, calculated at 18% per annum on the reversed amount of ₹18,000 from the date of availment.

• GSTR-3B Reporting: The reversal must be reported in GSTR-3B under Table 4(B)(2) (Others - Reclaimable Reversals).

• Right to Re-avail ITC: Upon eventual resolution of the dispute and payment to the supplier, you can reclaim the ₹18,000 ITC under Table 4(A)(5) and report in Table 4(D)(1) without any time limit under Section 16(4).
```

### Phase 4: Mathematical & Excel Spreadsheet Formulas
- **Days Outstanding**:
  ```excel
  =CalculationDate - InvoiceDate
  ```
- **Rule 37 Mandatory Reversal Amount**:
  ```excel
  =IF(DaysElapsed > 180, ProportionalITC, 0)
  ```
- **Section 50(1) 18% Annual Interest Liability**:
  ```excel
  =ROUND(MandatoryReversal * 18% * (DaysElapsed / 365), 2)
  ```
- **Re-Availment Ledger Status**:
  ```excel
  =IF(ActualSettlementMade="YES", "RECLAIMABLE_TABLE_4A5", "HOLD_IN_REVERSAL")
  ```
