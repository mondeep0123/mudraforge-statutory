# CBIC Circular No. 47/21/2018-GST: Composite and Mixed Supplies Guidance

## 1. Statutory Context & Principles of Bundling
Section 8 of the CGST Act, 2017 provides the statutory mechanism for resolving the taxability of bundled transactions. 

Transactions frequently comprise multiple supplies of goods or services provided under a unified commercial engagement. Determining whether such bundled supplies represent a Composite Supply under Section 8(a) or a Mixed Supply under Section 8(b) is essential for correct tax rate application.

## 2. Test of Composite Supply (Naturally Bundled)
Under Section 2(30), a composite supply comprises two or more taxable supplies of goods or services or both, which are naturally bundled and supplied in conjunction with each other in the ordinary course of business, one of which is a principal supply.

### Criteria for Determining Natural Bundling:
1. **Consumer Expectation & Trade Practice**: The perception of the consumer or the normal practice of the trade is that the package of goods and services is provided together (e.g., supply of laptop with warranty and charger, or passenger air transport with baggage handling).
2. **Integral Nature**: One element is clearly ancillary to the predominant element; the ancillary element cannot be realistically enjoyed without the principal supply.
3. **Principal Element**: One component represents the primary aim of the customer (principal supply under Section 2(90)).

Tax Treatment: The entire bundled transaction is taxed at the rate applicable to the Principal Supply.

## 3. Test of Mixed Supply (Single Price for Distinct Supplies)
Under Section 2(74), a mixed supply comprises two or more individual supplies of goods or services, or any combination thereof, made in conjunction with each other by a taxable person for a single price where such supply does not constitute a composite supply.

### Key Factors:
1. The goods or services are not naturally bundled in the ordinary course of business.
2. The items can be easily supplied and purchased independently.
3. A single, undivided price is charged for the package.

Tax Treatment: The entire transaction is taxed at the Highest Rate applicable to any of the individual components.

## 4. Itemized Invoicing Safe Harbor
Where individual goods and services are contracted with independent itemized prices and are not naturally bundled, each component retains its own statutory tax rate, provided the transaction is not artificially structured to evade tax on a composite whole.
