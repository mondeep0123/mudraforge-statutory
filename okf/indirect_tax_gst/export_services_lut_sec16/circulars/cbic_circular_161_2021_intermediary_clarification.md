# CBIC Circular No. 161/17/2021-GST: Clarifications on Intermediary Services

## 1. Statutory Background
Section 2(13) of the Integrated Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 defines an "intermediary" as a broker, an agent or any other person, by whatever name called, who arranges or facilitates the supply of goods or services or both, or securities, between two or more persons, but does not include a person who supplies such goods or services or both or securities on his own account.

Under Section 13(8)(b) of the IGST Act, the place of supply for intermediary services is deemed to be the location of the supplier. Consequently, even if the service recipient is located outside India and payment is received in convertible foreign exchange, the transaction fails condition (iii) of Section 2(6) (which mandates that the place of supply must be outside India), and therefore cannot qualify as a zero-rated export of service under Section 16.

## 2. Key Tests to Determine Intermediary vs Principal
The Circular establishes four definitive prerequisites before a service provider can be categorized as an intermediary:
1. **Minimum Three Parties**: An intermediary does not participate in the core supply of goods/services directly to the recipient on its own account, but arranges or facilitates the main supply between two other distinct principals.
2. **Two Distinct Supplies**: There must exist (a) the main supply between the two principals, and (b) the ancillary supply of arrangement/facilitation provided by the intermediary to one of the principals.
3. **Character of Agent/Broker**: The intermediary acts in an agency or facilitator capacity without altering the nature or value of the underlying service.
4. **Exclusion of Services on Own Account**: Where the service provider supplies software development, IT services, back-office data processing, or engineering design directly to an overseas client on a principal-to-principal basis, it supplies services on its own account. Sub-contracting of services on a principal-to-principal basis is NOT an intermediary service.

## 3. Impact on Zero-Rated Export Eligibility under LUT
1. If the entity provides services on its own account directly to the foreign client, the place of supply is determined under the default rule of Section 13(2) (location of recipient outside India), satisfying all five conditions of Section 2(6).
2. The supply is zero-rated under Section 16(1)(a) and can be exported without payment of IGST under a valid Letter of Undertaking (LUT) in Form GST RFD-11.
3. The exporter retains full statutory entitlement to claim refund of unutilized input tax credit under Section 54(3) of the CGST Act.
