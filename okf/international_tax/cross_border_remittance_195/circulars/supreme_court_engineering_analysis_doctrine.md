# Supreme Court of India: Engineering Analysis Centre of Excellence v. CIT (2021)

## 1. Issue & Judicial Dispute
In a landmark ruling delivered on 2nd March 2021 (Civil Appeal Nos. 8733-8734 of 2018), a three-judge bench of the Supreme Court resolved a decades-long dispute regarding whether payments by Indian resident end-users or distributors for foreign software products and cloud services constitute "Royalty" liable to withholding tax under Section 195.

## 2. Distinction between "Copyright" and "Copyrighted Article"
The Supreme Court held that there is a fundamental legal difference between:
1. **Transfer of a copyright**: The transfer of exclusive intellectual property rights to reproduce, modify, or commercially exploit the underlying code.
2. **Transfer of a copyrighted article**: The granting of a mere right to use a computer program or software application (such as standard shrink-wrapped software or off-the-shelf software).

A non-exclusive, non-transferable license granted to an end-user to use a software program does not involve the transfer of any copyright under Section 14 of the Copyright Act, 1957.

## 3. Application to Cloud Infrastructure and SaaS
The doctrine directly applies to:
- Standard software licenses (shrink-wrap / click-wrap).
- Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) and automated cloud infrastructure (such as AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, GitHub, OpenAI API, and database hosting).
- Hardware bundled with embedded software.

Where the customer is merely accessing standardized automated infrastructure without acquiring proprietary copyright, source code ownership, or human technical service intervention, the payment represents **Business Profits** under Article 7 of the applicable DTAA.

## 4. Operational Withholding Consequences under Section 195
In the absence of a Permanent Establishment (PE) of the non-resident vendor in India under Article 5:
1. The remittance is not taxable in India as royalty or fees for technical services.
2. The applicable withholding tax rate under Section 195 read with DTAA Article 7 is **0% (NIL)**.
3. The remitter must obtain:
   - Valid Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) of the foreign provider.
   - Form 10F.
   - No-PE Declaration confirming the vendor operates from abroad without a fixed place of business or dependent agent in India.
   - Form 15CA (Part D / Part A) and Form 15CB as applicable.
