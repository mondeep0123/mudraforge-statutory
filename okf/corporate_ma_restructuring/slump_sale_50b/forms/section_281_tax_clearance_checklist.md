# Section 281 & Successor Liability M&A Tax Due Diligence Checklist

> **Statutory Basis**: Section 281 & Section 313 of the Income-tax Act, 1961; Section 85 of the CGST Act, 2017.  
> **Purpose**: Verification checklist for corporate restructuring, slump sale, and asset acquisitions to prevent statutory voidability and successor liabilities.

---

## 1. Section 281 Transfer Voidability Risk
Under **Section 281(1)** of the Income-tax Act, 1961:
Where, during the pendency of any proceeding under the Act, or after the completion thereof but before the service of notice under Rule 2 of the Second Schedule, any assessee creates a charge on, or parts with the possession (by way of sale, mortgage, gift, exchange, or any other mode of transfer) of any of their assets in favor of any other person, **such charge or transfer shall be void as against any claim in respect of any tax or any other sum payable by the assessee**.

### Verification Steps:
- [ ] **Pending Scrutiny / Assessment Check**: Verify whether any notice under Section 142(1), 143(2), or 148 has been issued to the seller.
- [ ] **Outstanding Tax Demand Check**: Search the Income Tax portal for outstanding demand notices under Section 156.
- [ ] **Assessing Officer NOC (Section 281 Certificate)**: Obtain a formal written No Objection Certificate (NOC) from the jurisdictional Assessing Officer certifying permission to transfer the undertaking.
- [ ] **Safe Harbor Exception (§281(1) Proviso)**: Confirm transfer is made for adequate consideration without notice of pendency of proceedings, or with the prior permission of the Assessing Officer.

---

## 2. Joint and Several Successor Liability (Section 313)
Under **Section 313** of the Income-tax Act, 1961:
Where a business or profession is succeeded by another person, the successor is jointly and severally liable with the predecessor for tax assessment and collection for the year in which succession took place and the immediately preceding year.

### Verification Steps:
- [ ] **Tax Indemnity Covenant**: Business Transfer Agreement (BTA) must contain explicit tax indemnity covering all pre-closing direct tax assessments, penalties, and reassessments.
- [ ] **Escrow Holdback**: Secure an escrow reserve or holdback (typically 10–20% of consideration) for 12–24 months until pre-acquisition assessment years reach statutory limitation.

---

## 3. GST Successor Liability (CGST Section 85)
Under **Section 85(1)** of the CGST Act, 2017:
The transferor and transferee are jointly and severally liable for any unpaid GST, interest, or penalty due up to the time of transfer, whether determined before or after the transfer.

### Verification Steps:
- [ ] **GST Compliance Rating & Portal Inspection**: Verify complete GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B filings up to the closing date.
- [ ] **GSTR-9 & GSTR-9C Annual Reconciliations**: Verify annual return reconciliations with audited financial statements.
- [ ] **Form GST ITC-02 Certification**: Obtain Chartered Accountant certificate certifying transfer of business with provision for transfer of liabilities, enabling electronic transfer of unutilized input tax credits under Section 18(3).
- [ ] **Going Concern Exemption Qualification**: Verify the transaction satisfies Entry 2 of Notification 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate) to preserve 0% GST treatment.
