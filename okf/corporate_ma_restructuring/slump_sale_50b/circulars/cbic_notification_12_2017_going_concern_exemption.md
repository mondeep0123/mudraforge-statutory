# CBIC Notification No. 12/2017: 0% GST on Transfer of Going Concern

## 1. Statutory Exemption Scope
Under **Entry 2 of Notification No. 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate)**:
- **Heading**: Chapter 99 (Services)
- **Description of Services**: *"Services by way of transfer of a going concern, as a whole or an independent part thereof."*
- **Rate of Tax**: **NIL (0% GST)**.

## 2. Mandatory Tests for "Going Concern" Classification
To qualify for 0% GST exemption under Entry 2 (and avoid triggering 18% or 28% GST on itemized asset sales), the transfer must satisfy four statutory criteria established across High Court and AAR precedents:

1. **Organic Functioning Unit**: The assets, operational staff, operational contracts, and supply chain must be transferred together such that the transferee can continue business operations seamlessly without disruption.
2. **Transfer of an "Undertaking"**: Transfer of standalone machinery, surplus land, or idle factory sheds without operational business apparatus constitutes a taxable supply of goods, not transfer of a going concern.
3. **No Values Assigned to Individual Goods**: The consideration must be a lump-sum amount for the enterprise / division as a functioning economic unit.
4. **Independent Operating Capability**: If only a part of the business is transferred, that part must be an "independent part" capable of running autonomously (e.g. an independent branch or business vertical).

## 3. Input Tax Credit (ITC) Transfer & Form GST ITC-02
When a business is transferred as a going concern:
1. **Unutilized ITC Transfer**: Under **Section 18(3)** of the CGST Act, 2017 read with **Rule 41** of the CGST Rules, the registered seller is permitted to transfer the unutilized Input Tax Credit lying in their electronic credit ledger to the purchaser.
2. **Filing Requirement**: The transferor must file **Form GST ITC-02** electronically on the common portal along with a certificate issued by a practicing Chartered Accountant or Cost Accountant certifying that the sale of business has been done with a specific provision for transfer of liabilities.
3. **Acceptance by Transferee**: The transferee accepts the Form GST ITC-02 on the GST portal, and the unutilized credit is credited to the transferee's electronic credit ledger.

## 4. Successor GST Liability (CGST Section 85)
Under **Section 85(1)** of the CGST Act, 2017:
> *"Where a person liable to pay tax under this Act transfers his business in whole or in part, by sale, gift, lease, leave and license, hire or in any other manner whatsoever, the transferor and the transferee shall, jointly and severally, be liable to pay the tax, interest or penalty due from the transferor in respect of such business up to the time of such transfer, whether such tax, interest or penalty has been determined before such transfer, but has remained unpaid, or is determined thereafter."*

**Due Diligence Requirement**: Purchasers must demand full GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, and GSTR-9/9C audit clearance certificates, along with tax indemnities in the Business Transfer Agreement (BTA).
