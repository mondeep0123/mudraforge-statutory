# CBDT Circular No. 04/2011: Guidelines for Grant of Prior Permission under Section 281

**Circular Reference**: Circular No. 04/2011 (F.No. 402/92/2006-MC)  
**Issuing Authority**: Central Board of Direct Taxes, Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance, Government of India  
**Date of Issuance**: 19 July 2011  
**Statutory Subject**: Guidelines for grant of prior permission under Section 281 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 to create a charge on or part with possession of assets  

---

## 1. Statutory Context & Purpose

Under Section 281(1) of the Income-tax Act, 1961, any transfer of specified assets (land, buildings, plant, machinery, shares, securities, bank fixed deposits exceeding INR 10,000) or creation of charge thereon during the pendency of any proceeding under the Act or before service of notice of demand is void as against any claim in respect of any tax or sum payable by the assessee.

Under the second proviso to Section 281(1), such charge or transfer shall not be void if it is made with the previous permission of the Assessing Officer.

In order to streamline the procedure, eliminate arbitrary delays, and provide certainty to corporate assessees engaging in genuine commercial transactions, credit syndications, and mergers & acquisitions, the CBDT formulated standard guidelines for the receipt, processing, and disposal of applications for prior permission.

---

## 2. Key Operational Guidelines

1. **Prior Application Requirement**:
   - The assessee must submit a formal application to the jurisdictional Assessing Officer at least **30 days prior** to the proposed date of transaction, creation of charge, or transfer of asset.
   - The application must disclose the nature of the transaction, the value of the asset, full particulars of the transferee/lender, and the exact consideration agreed upon.

2. **Disclosure of Pending Proceedings**:
   - The assessee must provide full particulars of all pending proceedings under the Act (including scrutiny assessments under Section 143(3), reassessments under Section 147, revision under Section 263, or appeals before CIT(Appeals), ITAT, High Court, or Supreme Court).
   - Any outstanding demands under Section 156 must be fully tabulated along with their collection/stay status.

3. **Conditions for Granting Prior Permission**:
   - **Where No Demand is Outstanding**: If there are no outstanding demands and the pending proceedings do not involve substantial anticipated tax liabilities exceeding the assessee's retained net worth, permission should ordinarily be granted within 30 days.
   - **Where Outstanding Demand Exists**: Permission may be granted if:
     - The assessee pays the undisputed demand or provides an unconditional bank guarantee from a scheduled commercial bank covering the outstanding and anticipated tax demand; OR
     - The assessee offers alternative unencumbered immovable property or assets of equivalent or higher value as security, with a formal charge registered in favor of the President of India / Tax Recovery Officer.

4. **Time-Bound Disposal**:
   - The Assessing Officer is mandated to dispose of the application under Section 281 by either granting permission or communicating reasons for rejection within **30 days** of receipt of the completed application.

---

## 3. Legal Consequences for M&A and Lending Transactions

- **Insulation from Tax Recovery**: Once prior permission is granted and a formal Section 281 Certificate is issued by the Assessing Officer, the transfer or charge cannot be declared void under Section 281(1) by the Tax Recovery Officer.
- **Lender Priority**: Mortgages and charges created in favor of banks or financial institutions pursuant to Section 281 permission are legally protected against subsequent tax attachment.
- **Corporate Acquirers**: In slump sales and business transfers, obtaining this certificate or ensuring that the transaction qualifies under the bona fide purchaser exception (adequate consideration without notice of proceedings) prevents the Tax Department from attaching the transferred business assets.
