# CBDT Circular No. 09/2020: Loss Carry-Forward & Shareholding Continuity under Section 79

## 1. The 51% Beneficial Ownership Continuity Rule (Section 79(1))
Under Section 79(1) of the Income-tax Act, 1961, a closely-held company cannot carry forward and set off accumulated business losses incurred in previous years if there has been a change in shareholding, unless at least 51% of the voting power on the last day of the current financial year is held by shareholders who also held at least 51% of the voting power on the last day of the year in which the loss was incurred.

## 2. Inapplicability of Section 79 to Unabsorbed Depreciation (Section 32(2))
A foundational tenet of corporate direct tax law is the distinction between accumulated business losses under Section 72 and unabsorbed depreciation under Section 32(2):
1. The Supreme Court in *CIT v. Virmani Industries Pvt. Ltd.* [1995] 216 ITR 607 (SC) held that unabsorbed depreciation of past years becomes part of the current year's depreciation allowance under Section 32(2).
2. Consequently, Section 79 applies **exclusively to business losses** and has no application to unabsorbed depreciation.
3. Even if a 100% change in shareholding occurs and all accumulated business losses are forfeited, the company retains 100% of its accumulated unabsorbed depreciation, which can be carried forward indefinitely and set off against any future business profits or other taxable income.

## 3. Statutory Carve-Out for Eligible Startups (Section 79(2))
To facilitate equity fundraising and venture investments in startups:
1. An eligible startup referred to in Section 80-IAC is exempt from the strict 51% voting power continuity condition.
2. The loss incurred during the period of seven (subsequently amended to ten) years from incorporation shall be allowed to be carried forward and set off, provided **all the shareholders** who held shares carrying voting power on the last day of the loss year continue to hold those shares on the last day of the set-off year, even if their aggregate voting percentage falls below 51% due to venture capital dilution.

## 4. Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) Carve-Out
Section 79(2)(c) explicitly exempts any change in shareholding of an Indian company arising pursuant to an approved resolution plan under Section 31 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC), after affording a reasonable opportunity of being heard to the jurisdictional Principal Commissioner or Commissioner.
