# CBDT Circular No. 13 of 2021: Statutory Clarifications on Section 194Q

## 1. Application on Threshold Limit (₹50 Lakhs)
- **Question 1**: How is the threshold of ₹50 Lakhs calculated in the first financial year of enactment?
- **Clarification**: Since Section 194Q takes effect on 1st July 2021, the threshold of ₹50 Lakhs applies to the aggregate value of goods purchased from a resident seller during the entire financial year. 
- However, tax shall be deducted strictly on payments or credits made **on or after 1st July 2021** on the incremental purchase value crossing ₹50 Lakhs.

## 2. Adjustment for Advance Payments and Purchase Returns
- **Question 2**: How should advance payments and purchase returns be netted?
- **Clarification**:
  1. **Advance Netting**: If tax has already been deducted under Section 194Q on an advance payment, no further tax shall be deducted when the invoice is subsequently credited in the ledger.
  2. **Purchase Returns**: If goods are returned before payment, tax deducted may be adjusted against subsequent purchases from the same seller.

## 3. Exclusion of Non-Resident Direct Imports
- **Question 3**: Does Section 194Q apply to direct imports from foreign vendors?
- **Clarification**: Section 194Q applies strictly to purchases made from a **resident seller**. Direct imports of goods from non-resident sellers (where the non-resident has no Permanent Establishment in India) are not subject to Section 194Q withholding.

## 4. Tie-Breaker Priority with Section 206C(1H)
- **Question 4**: If both Section 194Q (buyer TDS) and Section 206C(1H) (seller TCS) apply, which prevails?
- **Clarification**: Section 194Q takes statutory precedence. If the buyer is liable to deduct TDS u/s 194Q and has deducted it, the seller shall not collect TCS u/s 206C(1H).
*(Note: Section 206C(1H) has subsequently been formally sunset on 1 April 2025 by the Finance Act, 2025).*
