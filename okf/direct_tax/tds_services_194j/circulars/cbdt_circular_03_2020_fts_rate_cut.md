# Statutory Amendment: Section 194J Fees for Technical Services (FTS)

## 1. Background & Legislative Rationale
Prior to the Finance Act, 2020, both "Fees for Technical Services" (FTS) and "Fees for Professional Services" were subjected to withholding tax at the rate of 10% under Section 194J. However, payments to contractors under Section 194C were taxed at 1% / 2%, creating massive litigation where assessing officers attempted to reclassify contractor works as technical services to demand 10% TDS.

## 2. Statutory Rate Decoupling
To eliminate friction and harmonize the tax rates:
1. **Fees for Technical Services (FTS)** (other than professional services) under Section 194J(1)(a) are subjected to TDS at the reduced rate of **2%**.
2. **Royalty for Sale, Distribution, or Exhibition of Cinematographic Films**: Deductible at **2%**.
3. **Professional Fees**: Payments to lawyers, chartered accountants, doctors, architects, and notified professional bodies remain taxable at the standard rate of **10%**.
4. **Director Remuneration (Non-Salary)**: Taxable at **10%**.

## 3. Statutory Classification Test: FTS vs. Professional Services
- **Fees for Technical Services (FTS)**: Governed by Explanation 2 to Section 9(1)(vii) of the Income-tax Act, 1961. Means any consideration for rendering of any managerial, technical, or consultancy services (including the provision of services of technical or other personnel), but excludes consideration for construction, assembly, or mining projects (which fall under Section 194C) and consideration chargeable under the head "Salaries".
- **Professional Services**: Governed by Section 44AA(1) and notified gazette orders. Confined to services rendered in the course of carrying on legal, medical, engineering, architectural, accountancy, technical consultancy, interior decoration, authorized representative, film artist, or company secretary professions, as well as Information Technology professionals notified under CBDT Notification No. 116/2001.

## 4. Reclassification Risk and Statutory Penal Consequences
If an enterprise deducts tax at 2% treating payments as Fees for Technical Services (FTS), and the tax authority determines during assessment that the engagement constitutes "technical consultancy" under Section 44AA(1) requiring 10% withholding:
1. **Differential Tax Demand**: The 8% shortfall is recoverable from the deductor under Section 201(1).
2. **Mandatory Penal Interest under Section 201(1A)**:
   - 1% for every month or part of a month on the shortfall from the date on which tax was deductible to the date on which tax is actually deducted/rectified.
   - 1.5% for every month or part of a month from the date tax was deducted to the date tax is actually paid to the credit of the Central Government.
3. **Statutory Expenditure Disallowance under Section 40(a)(ia)**:
   - 30% of the entire invoiced expenditure is disallowed from being deducted against business income in the relevant financial year until the tax has been deposited.
