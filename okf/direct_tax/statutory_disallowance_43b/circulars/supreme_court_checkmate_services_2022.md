# Supreme Court of India: Checkmate Services P. Ltd. v. Commissioner of Income Tax

**Citation**: (2022) 448 ITR 518 (SC) / Civil Appeal No. 2833 of 2016  
**Bench**: Hon'ble Justices Uday Umesh Lalit, S. Ravindra Bhat, Sudhanshu Dhulia  
**Date of Pronouncement**: 12 October 2022  
**Governing Provisions**: Section 36(1)(va), Section 43B(b), Section 2(24)(x) of the Income-tax Act, 1961  

---

## 1. Statutory Controversy & The Question of Law

Prior to this landmark three-judge bench decision of the Hon'ble Supreme Court, extensive divergence existed across various High Courts regarding whether an employer who deposits employee contributions to Provident Fund (EPF), Employees' State Insurance (ESIC), or other welfare funds after the "due date" specified under the respective welfare statute (e.g., 15th of the following month under the Employees' Provident Funds Scheme, 1952), but before the due date for filing the return of income under Section 139(1) of the Income-tax Act, 1961, is entitled to claim deduction.

Certain High Courts (including the Delhi, Bombay, and Karnataka High Courts) had historically applied the non-obstante clause and the First Proviso of Section 43B to both employer and employee contributions alike, permitting deduction if deposited before the Section 139(1) tax return filing deadline. Conversely, the Gujarat and Kerala High Courts had ruled that employee contributions stand on a fundamentally different legal footing from employer contributions.

---

## 2. The Landmark Ratio Decidendi (The "Checkmate Doctrine")

The Supreme Court definitively resolved the conflict in favor of the Revenue, laying down the following governing principles:

1. **Fundamental Distinction Between Employer and Employee Contributions**:
   - **Employer Contribution (Section 43B(b))**: Represents the employer's direct business expenditure and statutory liability. Section 43B governs this category, and under the First Proviso to Section 43B, deduction is fully allowable if the contribution is paid on or before the due date for furnishing the return of income under Section 139(1).
   - **Employee Contribution (Section 36(1)(va) read with Section 2(24)(x))**: Does NOT represent the employer's own business expenditure. By virtue of Section 2(24)(x), any sum received by the assessee from his employees as contribution to any provident fund, superannuation fund, or other fund is statutorily treated as the *income of the employer*.
   
2. **Strict Time Limit Under Section 36(1)(va)**:
   - Section 36(1)(va) provides a specific conditional deduction: the employer is permitted to deduct this deemed income *only if* such sum is credited by the assessee to the employee's account in the relevant fund on or before the "due date" specified under the relevant welfare legislation (e.g. 15th of the following month).
   
3. **Inapplicability of Section 43B First Proviso to Employee Contributions**:
   - The non-obstante clause in Section 43B ("Notwithstanding anything contained in any other provision of this Act...") operates to override provisions that would otherwise allow deductions (such as mercantile accounting under Section 28 or Section 37). It cannot operate to confer a deduction that has been statutorily forfeited under Section 36(1)(va).
   - The First Proviso to Section 43B applies strictly and exclusively to sums referred to in clauses (a) through (g) of Section 43B. It has zero application to employee contributions governed by Section 36(1)(va).

4. **Permanent Disallowance on Late Deposit**:
   - If employee contributions are deposited even one day past the 15th (or the statutorily extended due date under the EPF/ESI Act), the deduction under Section 36(1)(va) is irrevocably lost.
   - The amount remains treated as taxable business income of the employer under Section 2(24)(x) for that assessment year, and no deduction can be claimed in any subsequent year upon payment.

5. **Declaratory Nature of Finance Act 2021 Amendments**:
   - Finance Act, 2021 inserted *Explanation 2* to Section 43B and *Explanation 5* to Section 36(1)(va), explicitly clarifying that Section 43B never applied and shall never apply to sums referred to in Section 36(1)(va).
   - The Supreme Court affirmed that these explanations are clarificatory and declaratory of the pre-existing statutory position.

## 3. Practical Compliance Rule for Accounts Payable & Payroll

For automated accounting and statutory auditing:

| Parameter | Employer Welfare Contribution | Employee Welfare Contribution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Governing Section** | Section 43B(b) | Section 36(1)(va) read with Section 2(24)(x) |
| **Applicable Statutory Cutoff** | Section 139(1) ITR Filing Due Date (e.g. Oct 31) | 15th of the following month (EPF/ESIC statutory deadline) |
| **Consequence if Paid Past Due Date** | Timing difference: Disallowed in FY of accrual; allowable upon actual payment in subsequent FY | Permanent disallowance: Added to taxable income under Sec 2(24)(x); permanently forfeited |
| **Tax Audit Reporting** | Form 3CD Clause 26 | Form 3CD Clause 20(b) |
